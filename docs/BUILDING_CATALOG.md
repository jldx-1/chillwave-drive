# CHILLWAVE DRIVE

Master 3D Building Asset Catalog — 100 Unique Designs

Global Modeling Requirements

All 100 buildings must belong to the same futuristic nighttime city and use consistent world scale, physically based materials, and environmental lighting.

The architecture should combine contemporary skyscraper engineering with cyberpunk technology and chillwave visual influences.

Primary material palette: Blue and smoke-tinted glass, charcoal aluminum, brushed titanium, dark steel, pale architectural concrete, colored composite panels, translucent glazing, and integrated LED lighting.

Lighting palette: Cool white and amber interiors, cyan architectural accents, magenta commercial signage, occasional purple facade lighting, and warm street-level illumination.

Important modeling rules:

Model major architectural shapes as real 3D geometry, including building setbacks, projecting structures, recessed entrances, balconies, canopy frames, and rooftop equipment.

Use facade modules, trim sheets, normal maps, and instancing for secondary detail.

Avoid perfect repetition in window lighting and sign placement.

Use believable structural connections and support geometry.

Give every building a distinct silhouette, particularly those visible against the skyline.

Maintain recognizable front, side, rear, and rooftop geometry.

Include ground-floor details that remain convincing from the chase-camera perspective.

Give illuminated objects realistic surrounding light response rather than relying entirely on emissive surfaces.

Use separate meshes or material groups for glass, structural framing, cladding, signage, and architectural lighting.

Author LOD variants or equivalent procedural simplifications for long-distance rendering.

The following 100 designs are individual buildings, not merely texture variations.

PRODUCTION AND PLACEMENT GUIDELINES

A. Architectural Variation

Do not place these buildings in numeric order along the road.

Mix architectural families to form believable districts. Tall glass towers can sit beside shorter commercial buildings, while residential blocks and entertainment structures occupy adjoining streets.

Allow wide podiums to alternate with narrow towers, creating visual rhythm.

Avoid adjacent buildings having identical height, width, window patterns, or facade materials.

B. Chase-Camera Visibility

Most gameplay takes place at road level.

Consequently, the lower 5–10 floors of buildings visible near the player should receive the greatest geometric and material attention.

Model storefronts, structural columns, facades, balconies, signs, window frames, canopies, and entryways with sufficient detail to survive close inspection.

Distant upper floors can use simplified geometry while preserving building silhouette, window organization, and major architectural features.

C. Billboard and Neon Distribution

Not every building should be covered in digital advertisements.

Concentrate giant billboards on commercial, hospitality, and entertainment buildings.

Allow corporate towers to feature primarily glass, structural detailing, and restrained lighting.

Residential buildings should favor naturally illuminated windows.

Industrial buildings should use functional illumination rather than decorative neon.

The combination of brightly illuminated and comparatively dark buildings is essential for believable cyberpunk contrast.

D. Ground-Floor Variation

Provide multiple ground-floor treatments, including:

Glass office lobbies.

Recessed retail storefronts.

Covered pedestrian arcades.

Commercial malls.

Restaurant entrances.

Parking structures.

Hotel entrances.

Technical service bays.

Residential lobbies.

Civic entrances.

Landscaped plazas.

Raised terraces and planters.

Avoid a single generic commercial ground-floor module for all 100 buildings.

E. Rooftop Variety

Rooftops should feature different combinations of:

Mechanical penthouses.

HVAC housings.

Screened service equipment.

Communications antennas.

Architectural crowns.

Illuminated spires.

Maintenance railings.

Planted terraces.

Rooftop lounges.

Decorative lighting structures.

Use believable access and support structures.

F. Lighting Integration

Use building-specific lighting rather than a universal neon outline shader.

Glass should reflect nearby environmental colors.

Window interiors should appear occupied through varied illumination.

Terraces and entrance areas should receive localized lighting.

Illuminated signs should cast plausible reflected light onto adjacent facade surfaces.

Keep many areas dark enough for contrast and visual depth.

G. Procedural Construction

Where possible, create reusable systems for:

Curtain-wall windows.

Structural facade grids.

Balcony modules.

Recessed storefronts.

Glass entrance doors.

Canopy structures.

Roof equipment.

LED lighting strips.

Sign support structures.

Terrace planters.

Vertical architectural fins.

Reusing component systems is encouraged.

Reusing identical overall building silhouettes excessively is not.

H. Level-of-Detail Strategy

Use at least three practical detail tiers:

Near-field: Full architectural geometry for the visible building base, high-quality materials, detailed windows, entrances, and lighting.

Mid-field: Preserve major facade relief, balconies, sign supports, windows, and structural components while reducing small geometry.

Far-field: Preserve the building silhouette, major setbacks, distinctive crowns, primary window patterns, and prominent lighting. Use efficient geometry and materials.

Use instancing, batching, appropriate culling, and material reuse to preserve real-time driving performance.

I. Final Visual Objective

The resulting city must resemble a complex, highly developed futuristic metropolis.

From the standard chase camera, the player should see many distinct building shapes, detailed commercial ground floors, reflective glass towers, illuminated windows, prominent architecture, realistic structural depth, and a richly layered skyline.

The city should feel carefully constructed rather than assembled from repeated geometric blocks.

The core requirement is architectural sophistication first, sophisticated material and lighting response second, and decorative neon third.

All 100 designs should contribute to that visual identity while remaining suitable for a performant real-time 3D racing game.
